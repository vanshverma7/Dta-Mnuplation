Hi Marcus,
Just wanted to clarify a few points ahead of the KPMG walkthrough.
Membership numbers are obtained from REP 265 and average contributions from REP 151B. REP 151B is used for MI Data for Finance and Accounts BAU, providing a summary of various statistics such as highest contribution in the last month, number of employers completing their first scheduled contribution, and other key metrics. REP 265 is primarily used for the Charging report (shared with Robin Lewis), covering items such as total number of members and supporting TCS charge calculations.
Following the download of these files, the data is processed via an R script which merges the datasets, applies minor transformations (e.g. column renaming), and converts the output into an .rdata file. This is then stored on the O: drive and made available to the Analytics/BI teams and wider Nest for use.
In addition to this, other reports such as MFR are also extracted. MFR is used to deliver AUM figures on a monthly basis to the Finance team. There are no major transformations applied to this file; the Data team produces an MFR .rdata file, which is then accessed by the BI team to create summaries before being shared with Finance.
In terms of validation, as we don’t have an independent secondary source, we compare outputs against the previous month’s summary using an R script to identify any anomalies or spikes. I can walk KPMG through this process during the session.
I also wanted to check the expected scope for the walkthrough:

Should this include demonstrating how we access systems (e.g. Business Objects/SFTP), or focus only on how the files are obtained and processed?
Should we limit this to REP 265 and REP 151B, or also cover other reports such as MFR (used for AUM reporting)?
For BI-owned outputs (e.g. charging/AUM summaries), these are handled by the BI team. If this falls within scope, we may need to involve someone from BI (likely Harsh or Clothilde) to walk through their part of the process.

Happy to align on this before the call.
Thanks,
Vansh

As we don't have a secondary source to cross-verify the numbers, we validate them by comparing the summary with the previous month's summary using an R script, which can be demonstrated during the walkthrough.
