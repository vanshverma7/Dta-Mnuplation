Hi Sam,

Thanks for reaching out – great to hear you've been exploring this space too, and the context on your module is really interesting given what we're working on!

On your point about AI as a use case opportunity and the focus on explainability and quality assurance – it really resonates. Honestly, my view is that AI has become something of a hot word in organisations; it gets mentioned in meetings and strategies without people really using it to its full potential or understanding its practical usability. What I find exciting about a project like this is that we can actually put AI to work in a meaningful way, powering the fantastic analytical work the team already does and matching our wider ambitions rather than just talking about it.

The project is coming along well. Here's where we are:

For the AI tooling, we're planning to use Claude (specifically the Opus 4 model via Copilot 365), which has been impressive for coding tasks. One thing we're really leaning into is the idea of 'AI as a teacher' – rather than just using it to automate the conversion, we want analysts to engage with the output, understand it, and build their Python skills in the process. We think this approach fits well with DACI's wider objective of moving to Python over the next year or so, as it helps people skill up as they go rather than just handing them converted code.

On the point about reproducing analytical work in new data environments – this is actually where the transition to Python feels most critical. Once the JDT gold layer project completes and we have a working enterprise data storage that the team can properly query and build from, we'll need to be in a position where our analytical work is already running in Python. Getting ahead of that now means we won't be scrambling to convert everything at the point when the infrastructure is ready to use.

On the planning side, Josie and I are currently scoping things out. Josie has already sent out a survey to get a sense of who has 365 licences, Python installed, VS Code set up, and prior Python experience – which will help us tailor the approach. We're also looking at a workshop as the first step. Michael will lead on the 'why Python' piece (benefits, strategic rationale), and then Josie and I will walk through live working examples – Josie from an analytics perspective and me from the data conversion side. The goal is to keep it practical and accessible, not turn it into a Python training course. It's more about showing people how to use AI as a resource to do the conversion themselves.

One thing that's come out of early conversations with the analytics team is that they're genuinely excited – and in particular, they've flagged using AI to annotate code for documentation and QA purposes as something they're keen on. It's easy to see why; this is an area where AI can save critical hours on what is otherwise painstaking work. That said, an important part of the workshop will be reinforcing that human oversight over any AI output is non-negotiable. The code, the outputs, the decisions – these are owned by us, not the AI. If something goes wrong, AI doesn't take the blame; the person who used and signed off on it does. We want to make sure that responsibility is clearly understood and that people treat AI as a powerful tool in their hands, not a shortcut that absolves them of ownership.

Looking further ahead, we have a few additional options we're excited about. One is integrating an AI agent directly within Teams for quicker, day-to-day convenience. Another – and this is something I'm particularly keen on – is making use of our enterprise GitHub Copilot access. My vision is that once DACI is more comfortable with Python and ready to build more complex things, GitHub Copilot becomes a really powerful tool for that next stage of development. For now though, we're keeping it simple and working with what's immediately in hand, so these remain future-scoped rather than part of the initial rollout.
